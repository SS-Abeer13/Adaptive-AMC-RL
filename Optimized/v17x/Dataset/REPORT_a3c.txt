5. KEY FINDINGS & LATENCY BUDGET COMPLIANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Clean Apples-to-Apples Runtime Environment:
     Benchmarking all baselines (Fixed, Context-joint, OLLA) alongside NR2 within
     a single-threaded Python/NumPy runtime eliminates interpreter overhead disparities.
     The empty loop noise floor is negligible (median 0.10 us, p99 0.30 us, p99.9 0.50 us).

  2. Disaggregation of NR2 Execution:
     Splitting NR2 into State Construction vs Forward Pass reveals:
     - State Construction takes ~106 us (N=10) to ~124 us (N=100) median latency.
     - Forward Pass (pure inference) takes ~68 us (N=10) to ~136 us (N=100) in ONNX Runtime,
       and ~70 us (N=10) to ~165 us (N=100) in plain NumPy.
     - When combined (Full Policy Step: State + Forward + Action Selection), total median latency is:
       * N =  10: 186.10 us (ONNX) / 180.40 us (NumPy)
       * N =  50: 237.80 us (ONNX) / 255.30 us (NumPy)
       * N = 100: 284.90 us (ONNX) / 297.90 us (NumPy)

  3. 3GPP 5G NR TTI Budget Compliance:
     In 5G NR, typical subframe / slot durations are 1,000 us (15 kHz SCS), 500 us (30 kHz SCS),
     and 250 us (60 kHz SCS). On a commodity 1.7 GHz laptop CPU executing on a single thread:
     - NR2 full policy inference for 100 users (~285 us ONNX, ~298 us NumPy) easily completes
       within a 500 us slot (30 kHz SCS) or 1,000 us subframe (15 kHz SCS), utilizing < 30% of a 1 ms TTI.
     - For N = 10 users, full inference takes ~186 us, fitting comfortably within a 250 us slot budget.
