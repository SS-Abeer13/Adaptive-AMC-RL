R = answer from existing evidence (ledger ID); L = limitation, acknowledged in the paper; P = pending analysis or proposal (Checkpoint 5). Personas: (a) LA/3GPP systems expert, (b) deep-RL methodologist, (c) handling editor.

| 1 | **"+1.6 % is a small gain."** | a, c | Report both relative and absolute: +0.95 pp on-time (0.6126 vs 0.6031). The point of the paper is that the *published-style* gain vs OLLA (+12 %, R06) shrinks to this against calibrated rules (R33). Compare it with the static hindsight headroom: per-trace best offset +3.4 % over Fixed (R26). RL also exceeds Fixed by +1.87 % (R04). | 2 | **"This is just 'Hype or Hope' for wireless."** | c | Cite Doherty et al. (JOCN 2025) as the closest precedent. Our result differs: one regime survives a pre-registered test with project-wide correction (M04, G3a), and we explain *when* RL helps rather than whether it does | R |
| 3 | **"LOLLA reports +15–92 % over OLLA. Why do you see so little?"** | a, b | We do not dispute LOLLA; the settings differ (uplink with an immediate CRC outcome, round-robin, GPU NR stack, TDL channels). **Doppler is not the explanation**: our 3–30 m/s at 2 GHz gives f_d ≈ 20–200 Hz, which overlaps LOLLA's 50–200 Hz. **What the agent observes is.** LOLLA's agent sees per-slot PHY channel features. Ours sees delayed (2–8 TTI), noisy CQI plus HARQ state. LOLLA's own ablation fits this (Wang et al., arXiv 2606.23110v1, Table V(a), Sec. V-H, f_d = 100 Hz, TDL-A, 20 seeds): restricting its observation to HARQ feedback cut throughput from 254 to 185 Mbps, against OLLA's 171 Mbps (+8 % instead of +49 %). Presented as a reporting recommendation, not as a criticism. Neutral tie-in for the pitfalls section: gains depend on mobility regime **and** observation set, so both should be reported | R |
| 4 | **"Why not compare against LOLLA, SALAD or another published learned LA?"** | a, b | Different stacks: LOLLA is PPO on a GPU stack with a continuous offset; SALAD is ACK/NACK SINR tracking, with code available.
