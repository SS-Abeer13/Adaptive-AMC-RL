Scope: every section of `paper/manuscript/` (draft v1, commit `25634a2`), checked for (i) numbers against their source files, (ii) claims against the evidence and the frozen wording rules, (iii) statistical reporting, (iv) clarity and structure. The yardstick is what award-winning papers in the target venues do well.

## 1. Reference papers and what they teach

TMLCN has no best-paper award of its own; its papers compete for the **IEEE ComSoc Outstanding Paper Award**, judged on "originality, overall impact, and clarity of presentation". Two recent winners are close analogues of this paper, and two ML awards concern exactly our evaluation problem.

| Paper (award) | What it does that we should match | How the draft compares |
|---|---|---|
| Björnson & Sanguinetti, "Power Scaling Laws and Near-Field Behaviors of Massive MIMO and Intelligent Reflecting Surfaces", OJ-COMS 1, 2020 (ComSoc Outstanding Paper Award 2023) | Revisits widely used claims, names the hidden assumption under which they hold (far field), and shows exactly where they fail; also compares the new technology against an *optimised* conventional benchmark | Our thesis has the same shape (gains over OLLA hold when the comparator lacks the learner's cues). The conclusion was vague ("rarely, and by little") → rewritten as three explicit conditions; summary table added |
| Di Renzo et al., "Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces vs. Relaying: Differences, Similarities, and Performance Comparison", OJ-COMS 1, 2020 (ComSoc Outstanding Paper Award 2022) | Head-to-head comparison under equal conditions, with an explicit statement of when the new technology wins | Information table already gives "differences and similarities"; a summary table "where the learned policy helped" was missing → added (Table VII) |
| Agarwal et al., "Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice", NeurIPS 2021 (Outstanding Paper) | Interval estimates, score distributions over runs, probability of improvement; avoid relying on dichotomous significance; publish all runs | Hierarchical intervals were there. Missing: per-seed distribution against the strongest comparator, probability of improvement, a statement that binary rules only select wording, and a promise to release per-run data → all added |
| Ferrari Dacrema et al., "Are We Really Making Much Progress?", RecSys 2019 (Best Paper) | Tuned simple baselines; report the tuning effort; reproducibility | The draft claimed comparator tuning was "cheap" — false (see H2). Tuning budgets now reported |

## 2. Findings and fixes

Severity: 🔴 changes a claim · 🟠 number, definition or wording fix · 🟢 presentation. Full table in `conflicts.md` §H; ledger rows G6g, G6h, M07.

1. 🔴 **Combined-table search hit its cap.** One of four starts reached the 6-sweep cap while still improving; `cap_hit = no` had no source. Now reported (results, Appendix C); the build fails if this recurs. The capped start scored below the selected one, so the table is at most slightly under-optimised, in the learner's favour.
2. 🔴 **"Calibration is cheap" was false.** Single-cue re-calibration 2.9 h, combined-table search 24.6 h, versus 2.7 h to train and evaluate the ten M1 policies. Discussion and Cost rewritten.
3. 🔴 **Project-wide multiplicity was reported only for the easy comparison.** For M1 vs the combined table the Bonferroni-corrected p is 1.00. Added to protocol, results and abstract.
4. 🟠 Headroom paragraph compared percentage points with relative percent. Fixed.
5. 🟠 Rule R1/R2, the margin, its CI method, Claim B-500 and joint parity were used without definition. Defined.
6. 🟠 The programme text said all eight pre-registrations were frozen before any run; NR2 came later. Corrected.
7. 🟠 Fig. 4 showed only the comparison where 9/10 seeds win. The 5/10 row against the combined table is now shown.
8. 🟢 Internal labels (v14, v17) in the abstract and method; `\ref` misuse; integer separators; vague conclusion; keywords. Fixed.

New evidence added: probability of improvement 0.55 [0.49, 0.61] against the combined table (0.59 against Context-500), from `paper/analysis/g6_seed_stats.py`.
