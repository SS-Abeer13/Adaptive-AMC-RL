### Summary
This manuscript investigates physical-layer Adaptive Modulation and Coding (AMC) in a simulated 3GPP macrocell downlink under non-stationary interference bursts (+9 dB) and user heterogeneity, proposing an autonomous deep actor-critic architecture (Deep A2C v8) with scheduled per-user credit assignment and 3-frame autoregressive temporal state stacking ($L = 3$). The authors evaluate Deep A2C against seven baseline heuristics and oracles (Baseline, OLLA, Warm-OLLA, Multi-OLLA, Fixed, Fixed-Online, and FixedPU) across two scheduling regimes (Proportional Fair and Round Robin) and two evaluation horizons (200-TTI transient and 1000-TTI steady state). The headline result asserts that Deep A2C establishes a decisive "burst resilience moat" by delivering 2.23 Mbps during Markov interference bursts (+218% over Baseline), doubles Jain fairness from 0.413 to 0.851, and matches legacy OLLA tracking in steady state under formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue:** The manuscript completely lacks a Related Work section and bibliography, resulting in severe overclaiming of algorithmic novelty regarding autonomous reinforcement learning for link adaptation.
   - **Where:** Absent (entire manuscript; specifically lines 1–35, 43–49, 208–224, 288–294, and 416–424).
   - **Why it matters:** The paper frames "Strict Zero-BC Autonomous Learning" (training an RL policy for AMC without behavior cloning or teacher imitation) as a primary scientific discovery of v8 ("discovering link adaptation policies without oracle imitation"). However, casting Outer Loop Link Adaptation and AMC as an autonomous reinforcement learning problem learned from scratch is well-established prior art; for instance, Pulliyakode and Kalyani [1708.00994] formulated OLLA as an autonomous reinforcement learning problem using ACK/NACK feedback in 4G/5G systems nearly a decade ago, alongside extensive prior literature on Q-learning, contextual bandits, and policy gradients for wireless link adaptation. Without attributing foundational literature, the manuscript claims credit for establishing that RL can autonomously discover AMC operating points from random initialization, which is a mature and recognized capability. Furthermore, the paper cites only a single external article—Ahsen et al. [2604.20459]—and mischaracterizes its scope: Ahsen et al. developed a multi-offset OLLA framework specifically for higher-rank point-to-multipoint XR tethering groups over short-range sidelinks, not a general-purpose fast/slow dual-integrator for single-cell macrocells.
   - **What would address it:** Add a dedicated Related Work section and formal bibliography. Properly contextualize the study within the existing literature on reinforcement learning for outer-loop link adaptation (explicitly citing prior formulations such as Pulliyakode and Kalyani [1708.00994]), narrow the contribution claims to the specific credit assignment and burst adaptation mechanics, and accurately attribute concepts adapted from specialized architectures like Ahsen et al. [2604.20459].

2. **Issue:** The headline claim of Deep A2C superiority is falsified by the paper's own empirical data, which demonstrates that Deep A2C ranks 6th out of 8 schemes in cell throughput and is statistically significantly outperformed by simple static heuristics and OLLA.
   - **Where:** Section 2 Table (lines 124–207), Section 3 (lines 208–224), Appendix C (lines 563–580), and Appendix D (lines 614–630).
   - **Why it matters:** In the headline 200-TTI benchmark under Proportional Fair (PF) scheduling (lines 124–207), Deep A2C achieves a cell throughput of 3.55 ± 0.29 Mbps, falling behind `Fixed` (3.80 ± 0.37 Mbps, which statistically significantly beats A2C, $p = 0.014$), `FixedPU Oracle` (3.71 ± 0.36 Mbps), `Multi-OLLA` (3.66 ± 0.31 Mbps), `Fixed-Online` (3.64 ± 0.35 Mbps), and standard `OLLA` (3.58 ± 0.29 Mbps). More critically, in the 1000-TTI asymptotic steady state under PF scheduling (Appendix D, lines 614–630), Deep A2C (2.12 ± 0.21 Mbps) is beaten by every single scheme except Multi-OLLA: open-loop `Baseline` beats Deep A2C by +29.2% ($p = 6.1 \times 10^{-5}$), `Fixed` beats it by +6.0% ($p = 6.1 \times 10^{-5}$), and standard `OLLA` beats it by +5.0% ($p = 0.001526$). The text obscures this failure by selectively citing Round-Robin scheduling in the narrative (line 220) to claim TOST equivalence, while concealing that under the industry-standard Proportional Fair scheduler, Deep A2C is decisively non-equivalent ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.6577$) and strictly inferior.
   - **What would address it:** Extensively revise the headline claims and narrative. The authors must acknowledge that Deep A2C trades off overall cell capacity to inflate cell-edge throughput, explicitly discuss why an operator would accept lower aggregate spectral efficiency and increased computational complexity, and remove the selective framing that masks steady-state losses under Proportional Fair scheduling.

3. **Issue:** Deep A2C operates at an unviable initial block error rate of 53% (BLER1 = 0.530), indicating reward gaming rather than genuine link adaptation.
   - **Where:** Section 2 Table (lines 127, 202), Appendix C (lines 565, 572), Appendix D (lines 622, 641), and Appendix A Section 4.3 (lines 130–134, 302–304).
   - **Why it matters:** Standard 3GPP cellular networks are designed to operate strictly around a 10% BLER target ($P_{\text{tgt}} = 0.10$) to avoid control channel exhaustion (PDCCH/PUCCH), excessive HARQ retransmission latency, and buffer bloat. Deep A2C exhibits an initial BLER of 53.0% under PF (and 45.1% under RR, 38.8% in 1000-TTI steady state), meaning more than half of all transmitted packets fail upon first attempt. Inspection of the reward function ($R = \text{TBS}/30 - 0.5 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{drop}} - 0.05 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{NACK}}$) reveals that the NACK penalty ($\lambda_{\text{retx}} = 0.05$) is too weak relative to the spectral efficiency gain of choosing aggressive MCS levels. The RL agent has learned to exploit physical Chase combining as a crutch—deliberately overshooting MCS on transmission 1 and relying on accumulated SINR on retry 2. In a live network where retransmissions incur 4–8 ms latency and control signaling overhead, this policy would severely degrade quality of service.
   - **What would address it:** Re-tune the reward function with realistic HARQ latency and control channel signaling penalties (e.g., increasing $\lambda_{\text{retx}}$ or introducing a constraint-based penalty for violating the 10% BLER envelope), enforce an operational constraint that initial BLER must remain near 10%, and provide latency, retransmission distribution, and control overhead metrics alongside raw throughput.

4. **Issue:** The empirical data logs contradict the text regarding evaluation sample size, revealing that evaluations were performed on only 15–30 traces across 2 seeds instead of the claimed $N=100$ traces.
   - **Where:** Section 1 lines 87–88, Appendix B line 530, Appendix C line 560, and Appendix D line 610.
   - **Why it matters:** The authors claim in Section 1 (Table row C6) and Appendix B (Section 3.3) that Concern C6 from the prior review was "RESOLVED" by scaling the evaluation harness to $N=100$ independent channel realization traces, asserting a statistical power of $1 - \beta > 0.92$. However, the embedded raw execution logs in Appendix C explicitly state `Seeds=2 | Traces=30 | SimTime=200`, and Appendix D explicitly states `Seeds=2 | Traces=15 | SimTime=1000`. The study was not evaluated on $N=100$ traces; it was evaluated on 30 and 15 traces across only 2 seeds. This is an overt reporting contradiction that leaves the empirical findings underpowered and unverified against random seed variation.
   - **What would address it:** Execute the evaluation suite across the full promised sample size of $N \ge 100$ independent held-out channel realization traces and across at least 5 independent training seeds, update all statistical tables and figures with the resulting data, and reconcile the text with the actual experimental logs.

5. **Issue:** Deep A2C's burst agility and fairness gains are confounded by asymmetric, privileged state features withheld from all comparator baselines.
   - **Where:** Appendix A Section 4.1 (lines 104–122) and Section 3 (lines 214–216).
   - **Why it matters:** Deep A2C is provided with a 48-dimensional temporal state vector ($L = 3$ frame stacking of 16 features) containing privileged side-information: the internal integrated OLLA offset ($\Delta_{\text{OLLA}}$), true user velocity ($v_{i,t}$), uplink feedback delay ($d_i$), delayed interference burst indicators ($\hat{b}_{i,t}$), historical Proportional Fair rate, and scheduler context. In contrast, standard OLLA and Multi-OLLA are 1D scalar integrators operating strictly on 1-bit ACK/NACK feedback without velocity, delay, or burst indicators, while `Fixed` is a static scalar. Deep A2C effectively operates as a meta-controller on top of OLLA with access to rich channel side-information. It is therefore impossible to separate whether performance differences arise from reinforcement learning or simply from the massive disparity in input observability.
   - **What would address it:** Implement informed baseline variants that receive equivalent side-information (e.g., velocity- and delay-aware adaptive step OLLA, or burst-detection threshold triggers), and conversely evaluate an "ablated" Deep A2C policy operating solely on standard observable PHY inputs (ACK/NACK and reported CQI) to rigorously isolate the marginal value of the neural policy.

6. **Issue:** Multiple hypothesis comparisons lack family-wise error rate corrections, and equivalence claims are applied selectively.
   - **Where:** Section 2 lines 134–137, lines 176, 219–224, Appendix C lines 573–580, and Appendix D lines 623–630, 642–649.
   - **Why it matters:** Seven comparator algorithms are tested simultaneously against Deep A2C across multiple metrics without multiple-comparison corrections (e.g., Holm-Bonferroni). For example, the reported significance of `Fixed` vs. A2C ($p = 0.01397$, uncorrected) would be subject to adjusted significance thresholds ($\alpha / 5 = 0.010$). Furthermore, TOST equivalence is selectively declared in the narrative for Round-Robin asymptotic tracking while omitting that TOST overwhelmingly rejects equivalence under Proportional Fair scheduling ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.6577$ at 1000 TTIs).
   - **What would address it:** Apply formal Holm-Bonferroni adjustments across all simultaneous pairwise hypothesis tests, report adjusted p-values alongside unadjusted values, and maintain symmetric reporting of TOST equivalence across all schedulers and horizons in the executive summary and discussion.

### Minor concerns

- **Document Structure:** The submission is formatted as an internal engineering progress report / post-review audit rather than a standard archival IEEE Transactions manuscript; it lacks an Abstract, Introduction, System Model, Simulation Methodology, and Conclusion.
- **Overclaiming 6G Scope:** The title advertises "5G/6G Macrocells", but the simulation model strictly implements LTE / early 5G NR sub-3 GHz numerology ($\Delta f = 15$ kHz, 1 ms subframe, 15-CQI 3GPP TS 36.213 table). No 6G physical layer characteristics (e.g., sub-THz propagation, extremely large antenna arrays, or millisecond-subfraction numerologies) are present.
- **Inconsistent Equivalence Boundary Reporting:** The equivalence margin is stated as $\pm 0.10$ Mbps ($\pm 2.5\%$) in Section 2 (line 28) and Appendix A (line 318), but listed as $\epsilon = \pm 2\%$ in the accompanying technical report (`DATA_STATUS_REPORT_v8.md`, line 22).
- **Static Action Space Disadvantage:** The linear integrators (OLLA, Multi-OLLA) are structurally restricted to step sizes of 0.2–0.8 dB per TTI, requiring 11–45 TTIs to adapt to a +9 dB interference burst, whereas Deep A2C was granted an asymmetric action set allowing a single-step -6 dB drop. An adaptive-step integrator baseline should be included to ensure fair transient benchmarking.
- **Missing Figure Plots:** Figures 1 through 6 are referenced on pages 4–6 with brief descriptive captions, but no actual graphical plots, axes, or data visualizations are embedded in the paper text.

---

### Verdict
**Major revision** — The manuscript must resolve the severe discrepancy between claimed ($N=100$) and actual ($N=15\text{--}30$) evaluation traces, re-tune the reward function to eliminate the unacceptable 53% initial BLER operating point, incorporate essential related work on reinforcement learning for link adaptation, and re-frame all claims to reflect that Deep A2C is outperformed in overall cell capacity by conventional baselines under Proportional Fair scheduling.
