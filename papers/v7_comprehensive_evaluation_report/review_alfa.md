### Summary
This paper investigates whether deep reinforcement learning (DRL) can provide genuine state-conditional adaptivity for Adaptive Modulation and Coding (AMC) in a mobile cellular downlink under non-stationary interference bursts, user heterogeneity, steep BLER waterfalls, and HARQ chase combining. Evaluating seven schemes across a 12-point pre-registered campaign, the authors report a null result on their primary hypothesis: Deep A2C fails to outperform an oracle-calibrated, per-user-class constant offset rule (FixedPU) across all pre-registered points (0/9 wins). While Deep A2C maintains a +7% to +13% throughput advantage over standard Outer Loop Link Adaptation (OLLA) that survives target-BLER re-tuning, the authors conclude that this gain is merely a scalar setpoint shift—operating at an aggressive ~40–45% first-transmission BLER subsidized by HARQ—and that the optimal link adaptation policy in this environment class is a static constant offset rather than a state-conditional policy.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue:** Complete omission of the reinforcement learning reward formulation, MDP transition dynamics, and core training hyperparameters prevents replication and obscures why the RL agent failed to learn a state-conditional policy.
   - **Where:** Absent across Section 2, Section 3, Section 5, and Section 13.
   - **Why it matters:** The central thesis of the report—that state-conditional adaptation inherently provides no value over a constant offset in this environment class—assumes that the learning algorithm had sufficient expressive capacity and an appropriate optimization objective to discover such a policy. However, the scalar reward function $R_t$ driving Q-learning, Tabular Actor-Critic, and Deep A2C is nowhere specified in the text. Was $R_t$ defined as instantaneous throughput ($TBS \cdot (1 - \text{BLER})$), binary ACK/NACK feedback, or effective spectral efficiency penalized by dropped packets after exceeding `harq_max_retx`? If the discount factor $\gamma$ was set to 0 (myopic) or close to 0, the agent is mathematically incapable of learning anticipatory actions across the 250-TTI calm / 120-TTI burst Markov interference cycles. Furthermore, critical optimization hyperparameters—learning rates for actor and critic, optimizer type (Adam, RMSProp), gradient clipping thresholds, rollout/horizon length per update, batch size, and episode length in TTIs—are entirely omitted. In addition, tabular schemes are said to use a "35k-entry discretized state-action table" (line 577), yet the binning boundaries and discretization scheme are undocumented. These omissions render independent replication impossible and leave open the strong possibility that the null result is an artifact of an ill-posed reward or broken optimization rather than a fundamental limitation of state-conditional control.
   - **What would address it:** Provide the explicit mathematical definition of the reward function $R(s, a, s')$, including all penalties and scaling constants. Document the full hyperparameter suite in a dedicated table (learning rates, discount factor $\gamma$, optimizer settings, episode lengths, rollout buffers, and state discretization bin edges for tabular learners), and release the simulator and training code in an open repository.

2. **Issue:** The headline claim that RL matches the oracle-tuned baseline without per-deployment tuning is directly contradicted by the fact that parity required an oracle-initialized behavior cloning clone, whereas the uninitialized model significantly lagged behind.
   - **Where:** Section 6 (lines 405–423), Table 2, Section 11 (lines 604–610), and Section 12 (lines 621–623, 633–636).
   - **Why it matters:** The authors claim in the abstract and Section 11 that "reinforcement learning recovers, but does not exceed, oracle-tuned constant offsets in this class of environment." However, the evidence in Table 2 shows that parity was achieved *only* by `tier1b` (3.71 Mbps vs. FixedPU 3.66 Mbps, $p = 0.813$), which was explicitly warm-started with 150 episodes of behavior cloning (BC) supervised directly on FixedPU’s actions. Crucially, the subsequent 500 episodes of RL fine-tuning yielded zero additional throughput gain (+0.0 Mbps). When trained from scratch without oracle supervision (`tier1-nobc`), Deep A2C achieved only 3.54 Mbps—a 4.6% drop relative to `tier1b` and a 5.1% deficit against FixedPU ($p = 0.06$). With an evaluation sample of only 30 traces, $p = 0.06$ represents a severe statistical power limitation, not proof of equivalence. Stating that RL matches the oracle without tuning when only the oracle-initialized clone did so is an overclaim that conflates behavioral cloning of an oracle with autonomous policy discovery.
   - **What would address it:** Reframe the claims to state clearly that RL trained from scratch failed to match the oracle baseline (settling at a 5.1% deficit), and that the parity observed in `tier1b` is strictly an artifact of supervised imitation of the oracle comparator. Tone down claims of "recovering oracle performance without calibration" throughout the abstract and conclusion.

3. **Issue:** The simulation MDP and action encoding structurally handicap dynamic adaptation, artificially forcing the policy toward a constant operating point.
   - **Where:** Section 2.1 (lines 170–183), Section 2.3 (lines 190–196), and Section 13.1 (lines 664–678).
   - **Why it matters:** The paper concludes that Markov interference bursts (+6 to +12 dB) and user heterogeneity offer no exploitable structure for dynamic adaptation. However, the simulation architecture enforces two severe structural constraints:
     1. *OLLA-relative action bounding:* The action space is defined as an additive scalar offset to OLLA ($\Delta \in \{-5, \dots, +3\}$). OLLA itself is a slow integrator ($du/dd$) designed for stationary margins. When an abrupt +9 dB or +12 dB interference burst strikes, a constrained offset of $-5$ cannot instantly compensate for the massive SINR drop if OLLA’s internal offset has not yet wound down.
     2. *HARQ transmission freezing:* In Section 13.1 (`txStep_v7.m`), when a transport block fails (`H.pend(i) == true`), retransmissions are strictly locked to the initial transmission's MCS (`H.mcs(i)`) for up to 3 subsequent attempts, completely bypassing the RL agent's decision loop. If an agent enters an interference burst with an aggressive MCS, it is locked into repeated failures regardless of any state transition.
     Recent work in link adaptation has demonstrated that dynamic tracking of channel/interference variations and multi-offset or direct SINR inference from ACK/NACK history (e.g., [2510.05784v2], [2604.20459v2]) can achieve substantial throughput gains over static offsets. By confining the agent to a narrow offset around a sluggish OLLA integrator and locking out control during retransmissions, the MDP design structurally forces the agent to behave like an invariant offset, creating a self-fulfilling null result.
   - **What would address it:** Test an unconstrained action representation (e.g., direct MCS selection or wide dynamic offset range) and evaluate whether allowing the policy to adapt retransmission MCSs or fast-track burst entries enables genuine state-conditional gains over FixedPU.

4. **Issue:** Evaluation on only 30 held-out traces exhibits high seed sensitivity and insufficient statistical power to establish equivalence between policies.
   - **Where:** Section 4.1 (Table 1), Section 4.2 (lines 346–352), Table 2 (lines 420–422), and Table 3 (lines 482–486).
   - **Why it matters:** The authors explicitly note in Section 4.2 that evaluating under a fresh random seed altered the A2C vs. FixedPU comparison from a non-significant $-0.7\%$ ($p = 0.517$) to a statistically significant loss of $-4.4\%$ ($p = 0.006$). Despite discovering this high seed sensitivity, the subsequent Tier-1 diagnostics, state ablations, and external validations were evaluated on a single seed configuration across only 30 held-out traces. With $N = 30$, the test has high variance (e.g., Table 3 shows Deep A2C at $3.74 \pm 0.34$ Mbps vs. Constant Offset $-1$ at $3.61 \pm 0.32$ Mbps). Concluding that A2C is "statistically indistinguishable from a constant offset" ($p = 0.136$) or that `tier1-nobc` achieves "near-parity" ($p = 0.06$) commits the classical error of equating absence of evidence with evidence of absence. Failure to reject the null at $\alpha = 0.05$ with $N = 30$ cannot establish policy equivalence.
   - **What would address it:** Increase the evaluation sample size across at least 100–200 held-out traces over multiple independent training and evaluation seeds. Perform formal equivalence testing (such as Two One-Sided Tests, TOST) within a pre-specified equivalence margin ($\pm \epsilon$) rather than relying on non-significant p-values from difference tests.

5. **Issue:** Incomplete specification of physical layer parameters, channel generation seeds, and baseline AMC CQI mapping curves makes the wireless environment impossible to reconstruct.
   - **Where:** Section 2.2 (lines 184–189), Section 2.4 (lines 197–210), and Section 13.1 (lines 664–678).
   - **Why it matters:** In wireless PHY evaluations, link adaptation throughput is heavily dependent on carrier bandwidth, subcarrier spacing, resource block (RB) allocation per TTI, and the exact AWGN/fading mapping to CQI indices. While Eq. (2) specifies the carrier frequency ($f_c = 2\text{ GHz}$) and TTI ($T_{tti} = 1\text{ ms}$), the paper omits the system bandwidth, total RBs available, the modulation and coding scheme table mapping SINR to AWGN BLER, and the parameter values for the steepened sigmoid curves ($k \in [3.0, 5.8]$). Without these parameters and the implementation of `tbsFromMCSandRB` and `estimateBLER_v7`, external researchers cannot simulate the identical SNR operating regimes or verify the validity of the +2.5 dB Chase combining assumption.
   - **What would address it:** Add a comprehensive physical layer simulation parameter table specifying carrier bandwidth, FFT size, subcarrier spacing, RB grid allocation, noise floor/figure, the full MCS table with spectral efficiencies, and the exact parameterization of the BLER waterfall functions.

### Minor concerns

- **Inconsistent baseline percentage conventions:** In Table 3 (lines 481, 485, 493), baselines that underperform Deep A2C (e.g., Random Action at 3.02 Mbps vs. A2C at 3.74 Mbps) are listed as "+24.1%", whereas in Table 1, underperforming configurations are labeled with negative percentages (e.g., "-4.2%"). The sign convention should be unified and clearly defined (relative to baseline or relative to A2C).
- **Ambiguous caption in Table 1:** Line 255 states "N = 10, 5 seeds, 30 held-out traces", but Table 1 presents single aggregate values without reporting mean $\pm$ standard deviation across seeds, making it unclear whether numbers represent a pool or a single representative seed.
- **Unclear throughput scaling units:** In Section 13.1 (lines 670, 687), the code snippet calculates throughput as `tp(i) = TBS * 1e-3`. The text does not document the base unit of TBS (bits vs. kbits) or how this scales to the cell throughputs reported in Mbps.
- **Missing literature citations for OLLA and AMC standards:** The paper repeatedly references "industry-standard OLLA" and "classical 10% BLER design target" without citing foundational 3GPP specifications (e.g., 3GPP TS 36.213 / TS 38.214) or standard outer-loop link adaptation literature.
- **Missing tabular data for secondary metrics:** While Figures 11, 12, 13, and 14 plot scheduler sensitivity, Jain’s fairness index, edge throughput, action frequencies, and convergence curves, the exact numerical values for fairness and edge performance are omitted from all tables.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — While the report offers a commendably transparent post-mortem of why reinforcement learning failed to beat a fixed-offset baseline, the sweeping conclusion that state-conditional adaptivity has no value is undermined by the complete omission of the RL reward function and hyperparameters, an action/HARQ MDP formulation that structurally restricts dynamic adaptation during bursts, and reliance on an oracle-initialized clone to assert throughput parity under low statistical power.
